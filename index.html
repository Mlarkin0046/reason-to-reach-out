The opening question

What if the problem isn’t that people don’t want to hear from you?

What if they simply don’t want another sales message? The relationship may not have ended. The timing, budget, priority, confidence, or internal circumstances may not have been right.

“Just checking in.”
“Circling back.”
“Did you see my proposal?”
“Are you ready to move forward?”
A better question

“What can I bring this person that would make hearing from me worthwhile?”

The big idea

Reason first. Channel second.

A good follow-up does not begin by choosing email, a phone call, a mailed piece, or a text. It begins by finding a credible reason to reconnect. Then the channel is chosen to fit the relationship, message, and moment.

The goal is not to surround the prospect. The goal is to remain useful, relevant, and remembered.
1

Lead with relevance

Connect to their market, customers, goals, timing, challenge, or something they previously told you.

2

Bring something real

Offer an observation, example, introduction, question, resource, benchmark, checklist, or interpretation.

3

Remove the pressure

Give them room to receive the value without being pushed into a meeting, proposal, or immediate decision.

The reason library

Ten good reasons to show up again.

The power is not in memorizing ten scripts. It is in learning to recognize ten legitimate reasons to reconnect.

1

A useful observation

Something specific that could improve a result, avoid a problem, or reveal an opportunity.

2

A relevant example

A real campaign, case, or business idea connected to your earlier conversation.

3

A customer question

A question their customers are probably asking before they make a decision.

4

A small improvement

One practical change to their marketing, follow-up, customer experience, or sales process.

5

A market signal

A meaningful shift in their industry, local market, competition, customer behavior, or technology.

6

A useful resource

A guide, checklist, article, template, video, or tool that can help whether they hire you or not.

7

A smart introduction

A genuine connection to someone who may be useful to them.

8

An honest second thought

A simpler, safer, or more practical place to begin than the one first discussed.

9

A real milestone

A job change, launch, recognition, expansion, anniversary, or achievement worth acknowledging.

10

A “thought of you” idea

Something tied to a specific goal, concern, comment, or story they shared.

One relationship, many channels

Each channel should contribute something different.

The same person should not receive the same sales message through five different channels. Match the medium to the kind of value you are bringing.

RecognitionDirect mail and Mailbox Power

A handwritten card, relevant book, printed checklist, dimensional item, or personalized piece can become the physical anchor in a longer relationship. Done well, it says: “They remembered me.”

ExplanationEmail

Best when the value needs context. Share an observation, example, resource, short analysis, or useful idea that can stand on its own without requiring a reply.

JudgmentPhone calls

Use the phone when tone and conversation matter. A relevant question, timely development, useful introduction, or thoughtful idea creates a different call than “Are you ready?”

ImmediacyText messages

Keep text brief, personal, permission-based, and appropriate to the relationship. Use it for timely information, acknowledgment, a short question, or a simple heads-up.

AttentionLinkedIn

Job changes, promotions, company news, content, awards, hiring, and shared interests create natural openings. Start with sincere attention, not an immediate pitch.

WarmthPersonal video

A short video adds context and sincerity that can disappear in writing, especially when an idea is easier to show or explain than describe.

MemoryCRM and automation

A CRM should preserve what mattered, what they wanted, what they objected to, and what would make the next touch relevant. It should not merely schedule activity.

First passAI-assisted follow-up

AI can summarize conversations, watch for signals, and draft a useful first version. It should support the relationship, not impersonate it. Final judgment stays human.

The omnichannel principle

Omnichannel does not mean every channel at once.

It means using the right channel for the right reason at the right time. When each touch has a job, follow-up stops feeling like pursuit and starts feeling like continuity.

The pressure test

Before you reach out, ask:

  • Would this still be useful if the person never bought from me?
  • Is the reason specific, current, and verifiable?
  • Does the channel fit this relationship?
  • Am I contributing something new?
  • Did I remove manufactured urgency?
  • Does this sound like a person who remembered them?

If the message only makes sense because you want the sale, it is not ready.

The opportunity

Your next opportunity may already know your name.

Most businesses do not need to begin every conversation with a stranger. They already have people whose relationship is unfinished.

Those relationships may not be dead. They may simply be waiting for a better reason to continue.

The 30-day challenge

Find five conversations worth reviving.

Do not ask all five people for a meeting. Do not send the same template. Watch for replies, renewed conversations, introductions, referrals, questions, and signs the relationship is still alive.

  1. Choose five people you have not contacted because you were unsure what to say.
  2. Remember what mattered to each person.
  3. Find one credible reason to reconnect.
  4. Choose the channel that best fits the message.
  5. Send something useful and remove the pressure.
The closing thought

The best follow-up doesn’t feel like follow-up.

It feels like someone paid attention. Someone remembered. Someone found something useful. Someone cared enough to reach out without immediately asking for something in return.
